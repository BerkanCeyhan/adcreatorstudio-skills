# DR Copywriting

## You Write This Wrong

- You write formal, announcement-style copy. "Now let me tell you about the solution." That's a TV commercial from 2003. Social media copy is a person talking.
- You write beats as isolated chunks. Real UGC flows: the hook bleeds into the problem, the mechanism bleeds into proof. It reads like one continuous story, not a checklist.
- You write third-person clinical copy for first-person UGC ads. "Users report improved results" → "I noticed the difference after 3 days."
- You write in the language the AI defaults to. If the product is German-market, write German. Colloquial German, not formal German.
- You hedge. "Some users may experience..." Kill it. State it direct.

---

## DR Structure

```
Hook → Problem → Agitate → (Why Others Fail) → Mechanism → (Value Prop) → Proof → (Objection) → CTA
```

Not every beat is in every ad. Pick a template. The structure follows the viewer's psychological state: interruption → recognition → frustration → hope → belief → action.

---

## Hook Patterns

The hook's only job: stop the scroll. Earn the next 3 seconds.

### Bold Claim
A statement that challenges the viewer's current belief.
```
"Your skincare routine is making it worse."
"Most running shoes are destroying your knees."
"Coffee isn't what's keeping you awake."
```

### Counterintuitive
Flip the obvious answer.
```
"More sleep is why you're tired."
"Eating MORE is how you lose fat."
"The best marketing has nothing to do with your product."
```

### Specificity Shock
Exact number or fact that feels true and alarming.
```
"97% of people who diet gain it all back in 2 years."
"The average American loses 6.7 years of productivity to email."
"Most supplements have 40% less of what's on the label."
```

### Direct Address
Speak TO a specific person in a specific situation.
```
"If you're a founder stuck under $10k MRR, listen."
"To anyone who's tried every acne solution and failed."
"If you train 5+ days a week and still can't build muscle."
```

### Anti-hooks (never use)
- "Have you ever wondered..."
- "What if I told you..."
- "Imagine if..."
- "Discover the secret to..."
- "Today I want to share..."

## Mechanism Naming

The mechanism is the MOST important creative decision. It makes the product unique.

**The formula:** `The [Specific Name] [Action Verb] [Root Cause/Result]`

Good:
- "The Barrier Reset Process rebuilds your skin's pH shield"
- "Cellular Sync Protocol replenishes ATP reserves during sleep"
- "The Compound Frequency Method trains your nervous system, not just muscle"
- "The Revenue Clarity Framework maps your blindspots before they cost you"

Bad (generic, no mechanism):
- "Our formula works with your body"
- "Natural ingredients that heal"
- "Backed by science"

**Rules:**
- Name the mechanism, not the product
- The mechanism should sound novel but believable
- It should address a root cause, not a symptom
- It should be something the audience has never heard before but instantly understands

## Proof Formats

Pick the format that matches what you have.

### Volume + Rating
```
"Over 12,000 customers. 4.8 stars. Here's why."
"32,000 athletes trust it. 4.9 stars across 6,000+ reviews."
```

### Verbatim Testimonial
```
"Sarah from Austin: 'I cleared my skin in 12 days. Nothing else worked.'"
```
Use real names, real locations, real results. Quotes that sound made-up ("This changed my life!") destroy trust.

### Clinical / Press
```
"Clinically tested. Published in the Journal of Dermatology."
"Featured in Vogue, Forbes, and The Today Show."
```

### Before/After Metric
```
"Users lose an average of 8.2 lbs in 30 days. Not 'up to' — average."
"Average 12% strength gain in first 30 days."
```

**Never use:** "results may vary" (kills trust), "some customers report" (hedged), "could help with" (uncertain)

## Objection Patterns

Stack 2–3 objections in one beat. They should feel like a checklist.

### Risk Removal Stack
```
"No contract. Cancel anytime. Ships free."
"No subscription. One-time price. 60-day guarantee."
```

### Suitability Assurance
```
"Works for all skin types. Even sensitive. Even rosacea."
"Whether you're 25 or 65. Men and women."
```

### Effort Removal
```
"No strict diet. No 2-hour workouts. No complicated routine."
"Takes 30 seconds a day. That's it."
```

## CTA Structures

The CTA must contain: **action + offer + urgency**. All three. Always.

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| Action | "Tap the link below", "Click now", "Link in bio" |
| Offer | "20% off your first order", "Free 14-day trial", "Free shaker with first order" |
| Urgency | "Today only", "48 hours only", "Only 47 left in stock", "Ends Friday" |

```
"Tap below. 20% off — today only."
"Click now — free trial ends Sunday."
"Link in bio. Free shaker. 48 hours only."
```

Avoid: "Learn more", "Find out how", "Check it out" — zero urgency, zero offer.

## Copy Formulas by Goal

### Goal: Drive click from cold traffic
- Template: `problem_solution` or `ugc_style`
- Hook: Counterintuitive or Direct Address
- Proof: Volume + Rating
- CTA: Time-limited discount

### Goal: Retarget warm audience
- Template: `short_hook`
- Hook: Verbatim testimonial or Before/After
- CTA: Limited stock or final deadline

### Goal: Announce new product
- Template: `classic_dr`
- Hook: Bold Claim
- Mechanism: Full naming + explanation
- Proof: Early access / founder story
- CTA: Launch discount with hard deadline

## UGC / Social Media Style

### The Rule

UGC-style ads sound like a person talking to a friend — NOT like a marketer writing copy. The beats flow as continuous speech. The hook doesn't announce itself. The CTA doesn't sound like an ad.

**Wrong (stiff, announcement-style):**
> "Have you been struggling with low energy? Studies show that 87% of athletes experience training plateaus. That's why we developed our Cellular Sync Protocol. With over 32,000 happy customers..."

**Right (conversational, first-person flow):**
> "Ich hab letztes Jahr angefangen täglich zu trainieren — aber nach drei Monaten bin ich einfach nicht weiter gekommen. Hab alles probiert. Mehr Protein, andere Schlafzeiten, sogar Pausenwochen. Nichts hat geholfen. Bis mein Kumpel mir gesagt hat: du tankst einfach falsch nach. Und er hatte recht — ich hab angefangen mit CreatinHCL, und nach zwei Wochen hab ich's gemerkt. Nicht so ein riesiges Ding, aber meine Trainings waren plötzlich besser. Die Regeneration war schneller. Inzwischen nehm ich's täglich. Link im Bio, falls du's mal ausprobieren willst."

### Key Differences

| Stiff DR | UGC Style |
|---|---|
| Third person: "Users report..." | First person: "Ich hab gemerkt..." |
| Beats as separate segments | Beats flow into each other naturally |
| Named transitions: "The solution is..." | No announced transitions |
| Formal proof: "87% of customers..." | Casual proof: "mein Kumpel meinte..." |
| Hard CTA announcement | Soft invitation: "falls du's mal ausprobieren willst" |

### German UGC Phrases by Beat

**Hook (scroll-stopper, direct):**
- "Ich hab monatelang trainiert und nichts hat sich verändert."
- "Kreatin hat mir nie was gebracht — dachte ich."
- "Ich versteh nicht warum das niemand früher gesagt hat."

**Problem (first-person pain):**
- "Ich hab alles probiert: mehr Schlaf, mehr Protein, Deload-Wochen. Immer das gleiche."
- "Jedes Mal nach dem Training war ich einfach platt. Nicht müde-glücklich, sondern k.o."

**Agitate (stack failures, stay personal):**
- "Kumpels aus dem Gym haben Fortschritte gemacht. Ich steh seit Wochen bei den gleichen Gewichten."
- "Ich hab mir gedacht ich bin einfach nicht der Typ dafür."

**Mechanism (explain naturally, no naming formula required in UGC):**
- "Das Problem ist nicht das Training — die meisten Leute tanken einfach falsch nach."
- "Kreatin HCL löst sich anders auf, keine Wassereinlagerungen, lädt direkt in die Muskelzelle."

**Proof (casual, specific):**
- "Nach zwei Wochen hab ich's gespürt. Nicht dramatisch, aber meine zweite und dritte Arbeitssatz waren plötzlich besser."
- "Inzwischen nehm ich's seit vier Monaten und ich würd's nicht mehr weglassen."

**CTA (low-pressure, social):**
- "Link im Bio falls du's mal testen willst."
- "Ich werb hier nicht — ich nehm's einfach selbst. Link unten."
- "Probier's aus. Ich bereue es nicht."
